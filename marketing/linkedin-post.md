Students often find it hard to picture the brain in 3D from flat textbook pictures and MRI slices. So I built a tool to help with that.

**Neuroverse** is a free neuroanatomy and imaging lab that runs in the browser:

🧠 **Take the brain apart.** An animated exploded view separates the hemispheres, lobes, deep nuclei, limbic system, ventricles and brainstem, one step at a time.
📍 **Where's the lesion?** Read a clinical story, mark where you think the lesion is, then see the reasoning, the lesion in 3D and the scan. There are 8 cases, from Broca's aphasia to Wallenberg syndrome.
🩻 **Imaging simulator.** MRI (T1, T2, FLAIR, DWI, ADC, SWI), CT, PET and MR elastography, computed from tissue physics. A linked 3D brain shows where each slice cuts.
📚 49 conditions and 43 structures, each with references.

The anatomy comes from the MNI152 template and the Harvard–Oxford atlas. Every scan is synthetic, so it's safe to explore freely. It's built for learning and nothing is graded.

👉 Try it here (works best on a laptop): https://neuroverse-mu.vercel.app

I'd love feedback from students, clinicians and educators. What would make it more useful for your teaching or revision?
